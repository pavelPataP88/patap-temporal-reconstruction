# Critical reassessment of PATAP v0.4

## Status correction

The v0.4 final-validation report records a completed, frozen synthetic
experiment. Its raw files, protocol, controls, and measured numbers remain
historical evidence and must not be edited or deleted.

However, the result must **not** be cited as external validation, independent
replication, proof of a general raw-trace extractor, evidence for physical time,
or a scientific discovery. Its earlier phrase “hard validation” describes only
the frozen internal benchmark gate and is too strong for external scientific
interpretation.

The corrected classification is:

> **synthetic construct validation of a specified trace model**

It shows that the implementation behaves as its explicitly designed latent
world, observation function, and extractor contract predict. It does not show
that independently created computations produce traces satisfying that
contract.

## Why this matters

The synthetic generator, evaluator truth, observation function, and extractor
were authored within one research environment. The generator’s actual
operations determine realised-use truth, while normal traces are emitted from
the operations. This avoids direct serialization of a dependency field, but it
does not produce independent external evidence.

The simple unique-fingerprint rule is a conservative join:

    one visible producer -> candidate direct edge
    zero visible producers -> UNKNOWN
    multiple visible producers -> AMBIGUOUS

That rule is appropriate as a soundness-oriented baseline under its contract,
but it is not an algorithmic novelty claim. The high v0.4 score therefore
means only that the stated contract and implementation were mutually
consistent on a controlled world.

## What the controls establish

- record shuffling and opaque-ID replacement rule out those specific leakage
  routes in the synthetic implementation;
- trace removal shows that the implementation did not reconstruct edges from
  those removed traces;
- duplicate-producer cases exercised the AMBIGUOUS policy;
- false trusted traces produced false positives, demonstrating a known
  identifiability limitation.

These are software and construct checks. They do not establish that real
captures have the required artifact-instance semantics or that observations
will be reliable outside the authored world.

## What v0.5 established

v0.5 did not produce a valid external measurement. The preflight correctly
stopped because a pathname recorded during execution and hashed after the
build cannot identify the concrete bytes read or written at the relevant
operation. This is a measurement failure, not a negative scientific score and
not a successful external validation.

It also exposes an essential burden for any future test: no realised-use claim
may use post-execution pathname hashes as a substitute for artifact-instance
identity.

## Correct current claim

PATAP has:

1. a dependency-to-partial-order software core;
2. a controlled synthetic demonstration of a conservative trace-identifiability
   contract;
3. explicit UNKNOWN, AMBIGUOUS, and false-trace limitations.

PATAP does not yet have external evidence that real third-party computations
permit recovery of realised dependency order from the proposed restricted
non-temporal observations.

## Required path to a stronger claim

A future external result requires, before scores are inspected:

1. an independently auditable version-aware capture mechanism;
2. an evaluator/public data split derived separately from raw capture;
3. preregistered corpus, controls, success criteria, and extractor revision;
4. published coverage of unresolved observations rather than deletion;
5. comparison with relevant provenance methods at the same information
   boundary; and
6. independent replication.

A low-scoring completed experiment is a valid scientific result. It must be
reported as PARTIAL SUPPORT or FAILS, not rewritten through a new trace model
or extractor.
